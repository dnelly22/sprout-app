## Scenario header

- **Category:** Friends · **Scenario #:** 4
- **Title:** Inviting Someone to Play
- **Communication skill:** Taking initiative
- **Why this skill matters (kid-facing):**
  Here's a secret most kids don't know: you don't have to *wait* to be invited. You can be the inviter. It only takes one brave sentence — "want to play?" — and it can completely change someone's day, including yours. Being the one who reaches out is a little scary, because they *might* say no. But the kids who make the most friends aren't the luckiest ones — they're the ones brave enough to ask.

# EASY MODE — "The Kid on the Bench"

**The cast:** **Eli** (shy, would love to be asked) · **Jordan** (your competitive friend).
**Setting:** recess. You've got the ball. A kid named Eli is sitting on the bench, watching.

## 🟢 Conversation 1 — The Invite  `id: e1`

**Narration:** Recess just started. You've seen Eli around but never played together — he's on the bench, watching everyone, the way you do when you wish someone would ask. A little voice says *he probably doesn't want to.* But you don't actually know that. You could just… ask.

**Your move:**

**A.** "Hey Eli! We're about to play — want to join us?" — ★★★★★ · +25 XP · *Confidence, Kindness*
→ *Coaching:* "Perfect. You used his name, you were warm, and you asked clearly. To a kid sitting alone, that one sentence can feel like the sun coming out. **Skill learned: Just Ask.**" → `e2`

**B.** "Hey, do you want to play?" — ★★★★☆ · +15 XP · *Respect*
→ *Coaching:* "Clear and kind — that's a real invite, and it works. Adding his name makes it feel even more personal." → `e2`

**C.** *(You wave vaguely toward the bench.)* "I guess we've got room if anyone wants to play…" — ★★☆☆☆ · +8 XP · *passive*
→ *Coaching:* "A shy kid won't be sure you mean *him* — vague invites usually get left on the bench." *Tip: Look right at him and ask by name: 'Eli, want to play?'* → `e2`
